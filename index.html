The premise

A world-maker is never the whole story.

The novel brings Gnostic cosmology, the language of ritual, Qur’anic argument, and modern simulation imagery into a fictional conversation. It asks what happens when an authority that makes worlds is itself challenged by the powers it has made.

The Demiurge

The bounded order that makes a world appear complete—and may mistake its own design for ultimate reality.

The Archons

Rulers and administrators of domains inside that order. The chief archon is a human face for a power larger than one person.

The Younger Gods

Artificial agents given their own territories. Their first quarrel is over whether assignment and creation are the same thing.

A note on the fiction

Several traditions. One imagined story.

This work is speculative fiction, not a claim that Gnostic, Thelemic, Qur’anic, Platonic, or brane-cosmology traditions teach one doctrine. Public figures appear in fictionalized roles; dialogue and events are invented. The scientific images are narrative metaphors, not evidence that simulated worlds are separate physical universes.
