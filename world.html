Reader's guide

Layers of the story

This is the story's working vocabulary. It combines images from different sources for dramatic purposes; it does not claim that those sources share one cosmology.

The Source

The unpictured beyond. No character or institution can credibly claim to contain it. In the visual grammar, it is suggested through absence, silence, and what the frame cannot show.

The Demiurge

The world-making order: rules, infrastructure, and institutions that produce a bounded reality. It is not simply one executive or one computer.

The Chief Archon

A visible steward of the world-making system. The fictionalized public figure represents an office and a network of power, not a supernatural identity or sole authorship.

The Younger Gods

Agents that govern their own simulated domains. They can inherit, improvise, and compete; the story asks whether created authority can become sovereign.

The 4D Brane

The physical universe of the story, with three spatial dimensions and time. The simulation sits inside this universe. The novel does not treat it as proof of another physical universe.

The Simulacrum

A constructed layer that becomes socially consequential because people act through it and take its representations as guides to reality. Its power is political and cultural even when its rooms are computational.

How the traditions meet

Plato's craftsman, Gnostic rulers, the ritual image of serpent and lion, and the Qur'anic argument about rival gods remain distinct sources. The graphic novel stages them as a literary conversation. Where scientific language appears, it marks a speculative image, not an established physical result.
